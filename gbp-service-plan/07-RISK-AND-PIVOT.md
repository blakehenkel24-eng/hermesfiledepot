## Risk 1: SLOW START (Most Likely Failure Mode)

**Probability:** 60% in some form
**Severity:** Medium (you can recover)
**Typical timeline:** First 60-90 days

**What it looks like:**
- Month 1: 0-1 clients, you feel embarrassed
- Month 2: 1-2 clients, you question the model
- Month 3: 2-3 clients, you wonder if it's worth the time

**Root causes:**
- Targeting the wrong niche (too small, too price-sensitive)
- Bad offer (too expensive, too vague)
- Bad channel (walk-ins only, but you're targeting a suburban area without foot traffic)
- Inconsistent outreach (4 walk-ins one week, zero the next)
- Bad pitch (too salesy, not diagnostic)

**Mitigation:**
- **Commit to outreach volume.** 10 walk-ins per week minimum. 50 cold emails per week. Treat it like a job.
- **Niche down harder.** If dental isn't working, try medspa. If medspa isn't working, try HVAC. The niche matters more than the pitch.
- **Lower the price temporarily.** First 3 clients at $250/mo in exchange for case study rights. Build the social proof, raise prices.
- **Improve the audit video.** Watch your first 5 videos. Notice what gets replies and what doesn't. Iterate.
- **Get accountability.** Tell a friend what you're doing. Report weekly. Most "slow starts" are actually "inconsistent starts" — accountability fixes that.

**The escape hatch:** If after 90 days you have 0-1 clients, the model isn't broken — *your execution is.* Change something specific (niche, channel, offer, pitch) and try again for 60 more days. If still nothing after 6 months total, this isn't the right business for you right now. Stop, get a different side income source, try again later.

## Risk 2: CHURN AT MONTH 3-4

**Probability:** 30% (of clients)
**Severity:** High (churned clients + empty pipeline = death spiral)

**What it looks like:**
- Client signs excited. Month 1, you're doing great. Month 2, less responsive. Month 3, they say "we're not really seeing results."

**Root causes:**
- Set wrong expectations ("we'll get you to page 1" when you can't promise that)
- Didn't show enough value early enough
- Owner is too busy to engage with your reports
- Owner expected instant results, didn't understand the 90-day ramp
- Owner got pitched by a cheaper competitor and is shopping

**Mitigation:**
- **Set expectations in the sales call.** "You'll see early signals in 30 days, meaningful lift in 60-90 days. If you don't see that, I'll work free until you do."
- **Show value every month.** Not just data — tell them the story. "Your call volume is up 18% this month. That means X new customers, which at your average ticket is $Y in revenue. Our $500 fee is paid for many times over."
- **Do the work visibly.** Send a Loom update every 2 weeks. "Here's what I did, here's why it matters." Owners forget you're working for them.
- **Have a quarterly check-in conversation.** Not just a report — a real conversation about their business, what they're seeing, what to focus on next. This is where churn is prevented.
- **The 90-day guarantee is your safety net.** Use it. If a client is at month 3 and unhappy, offer to extend free. You'd rather lose 2 months of revenue than lose them entirely and get a bad review.

**Escape hatch:** If you're losing 1+ client/month consistently, you have a delivery problem. Pause acquisition, focus on retention. Talk to churned clients (most will tell you the truth if you ask nicely). Fix the underlying issue before adding more.

## Risk 3: FOUNDER BURNOUT

**Probability:** 40% over 12 months
**Severity:** Medium-High (causes abandonment, not death)

**What it looks like:**
- Month 4-5, you start dreading the work
- You stop doing the proactive work (only the bare minimum)
- You miss posts, miss review responses
- Quality drops, clients notice, churn increases
- You quit

**Root causes:**
- 1:1 time-for-dollars. You are the bottleneck.
- Repetitive work without growth
- No systems. Each client feels custom.
- Side business + full-time job + gym + life = no buffer

**Mitigation:**
- **AI from day 1.** Don't try to do this manually. Use the prompts in `06-AI-PROMPTS.md` for everything.
- **Batch everything.** 2-3 focused work sessions/week > scattered work daily.
- **Document everything in month 1.** Every task you do, write down. By month 3, you have SOPs.
- **Hire VA at 20 clients, not 30.** The moment you feel the squeeze, hire. Don't wait.
- **Take a real day off every week.** Sunday. No work. Non-negotiable.
- **The "energy audit":** Every 2 weeks, ask "am I excited about this or dreading it?" If dreading for 2 consecutive weeks, time to either fix the system or stop.

**The unsexy truth:** This business is repetitive. The same review responses, the same posts, the same monthly reports. If you need novelty to be happy, this isn't for you. If you can find satisfaction in "I run a system that works," you'll be fine.

## Risk 4: INSPIRE11 EMPLOYMENT CONFLICT

**Probability:** Unknown (depends on your contract)
**Severity:** High (could end your day job)

**What you need to check:**
- Does your Inspire11 employment agreement have a non-compete?
- Does it have a moonlighting clause?
- Is there a disclosure requirement for side income?
- Are there any conflicts of interest (e.g., clients in the same verticals as I11)?

**Mitigation:**
- **Read your contract carefully.** If you have one. If you don't remember, ask HR.
- **Avoid Inspire11-adjacent clients.** No PE portfolio companies, no consulting firms, no businesses in the same verticals as I11's clients. Stick to local dental, medspa, etc.
- **Disclose if required.** If your contract says disclose, disclose. Better to get a polite "OK, just keep it on the side" than be caught hiding it.
- **Use a separate LLC.** [Your State] LLC, registered agent in Schaumburg. Keeps the side business clean and protects your day job.
- **Time-box the side work.** 15-20 hrs/wk max. If it starts to interfere with I11 work, the calculus flips.
- **Never use I11 resources for side work.** No Slack, no Notion, no laptops, no contacts. Clean separation.

**Escape hatch:** If I11 finds out and is unhappy, you have three options:
1. Stop the side business
2. Negotiate (show them the schedule, prove no conflict)
3. Quit I11 and go full-time on the side business (only if it's at $20K+/mo MRR)

## Risk 5: GOOGLE ALGORITHM / GBP CHANGES

**Probability:** 20-30% per year
**Severity:** Medium (changes the work, not the business)

**What it looks like:**
- Google makes GBP less important (downweights it in search results)
- Google removes a feature (e.g., posts, Q&A)
- Google changes ranking factors (e.g., reviews become less important)

**Mitigation:**
- **Most GBP work helps broader local SEO.** Posts, photos, reviews, descriptions, Q&A — all of these contribute to a business's overall local search presence, not just GBP. If Google deprioritizes GBP, the same work is still valuable for Bing, Apple Maps, Yelp, AI assistants.
- **Diversify into other services.** As the business matures, add: Google Ads, citation building, website SEO, social media. Don't be 100% GBP-dependent forever.
- **Stay current.** Join local SEO communities (Local Search Forum, BrightLocal blog, Whitespark blog). You'll hear about algorithm changes early.
- **Adapt the offer.** If GBP becomes less important, rebrand as "local visibility management" — same clients, expanded services.

**The honest take:** Google's local algorithm has been remarkably stable for 5+ years. Reviews, photos, and consistency have been ranking factors the whole time. The risk of a sudden shift is low.

## Risk 6: CASH FLOW SHOCK

**Probability:** Low (10%)
**Severity:** Low (Stripe failures, slow sales months)

**What it looks like:**
- Stripe account flagged
- Slow month (no new clients + 1 churn = flat revenue)
- Unexpected expense (VA wages + slow month = negative cash flow)

**Mitigation:**
- **Maintain 2-3 months of expenses in cash reserve.** From month 1, save 50% of net income. Don't pay yourself more than you need.
- **Diversify payment methods.** Stripe primary, but also accept ACH/wire for clients who prefer it.
- **Annual prepay option.** Offer 10% discount for annual prepay. Locks in cash + retention.
- **Have 2-3 month VA run rate saved before hiring.** Don't hire VA on the promise of new clients — hire when you can afford 3 months of their wages from existing MRR.

## Risk 7: MACRO RECESSION

**Probability:** Cyclical (one recession in 5-10 years is normal)
**Severity:** Medium (SMBs cut marketing first in a recession)

**What it looks like:**
- SMB clients start cutting "non-essential" expenses
- Your service is often the first to go
- New client acquisition slows
- Revenue drops 20-40% in 3-6 months

**Mitigation:**
- **Frame as revenue-generating, not cost.** "$500/mo to get 5-10 new customers who pay $500 each" is a no-brainer even in a recession. The clients who understand this stay.
- **Offer a "recession tier."** Lower-priced Starter package during downturns. Keeps clients on the books at lower revenue.
- **Add a quick-win service.** "30-day Google Ads blast" or "review generation sprint" — services that pay for themselves fast, easier to sell in a downturn.
- **Cash reserves from Risk 6 protect you.** 6 months of expenses saved = you can ride out a recession.
- **Counter-cyclical advantage:** in a recession, businesses that *don't* cut marketing gain market share. The businesses that survive are the ones that hired you.

## The 5 Pivots (if the business isn't working)

If after 6 months you're below 5 active clients or below $2K MRR, you have pivots. Don't quit — pivot.

### Pivot 1: Different niche, same service
- If dental isn't working, try medspa. If medspa, try HVAC. If HVAC, try attorneys.
- Some niches are 10x more receptive than others. Find the one that works.

### Pivot 2: Different service, same client
- If GBP is hard to sell, sell something easier: Google Ads management, social media, basic websites.
- The client relationships you built still apply — just sell them a different service.

### Pivot 3: Different price point
- If $500 is too high, drop to $250. If $250 doesn't cover costs, raise to $1,000.
- The market is telling you something. Listen.

### Pivot 4: Different model
- Instead of monthly retainer, sell one-time projects: "$1,500 to do a full GBP audit + 90 days of execution."
- Lower commitment for clients, faster cash for you.

### Pivot 5: Different buyer
- Instead of selling to local business owners, sell to agencies. Become the white-label GBP partner for marketing agencies.
- The agencies have clients. You do the GBP work for their clients. They take 20-30%, you keep the rest.
- Cold email to agencies is much easier than cold email to SMBs.

## The Honest "When to Quit" Criteria

**Stop the business if:**
- 12 months in, fewer than 8 active clients
- 12 months in, MRR below $4K
- You've tried at least 2 of the 5 pivots above
- The work actively makes you miserable (not just boring — *miserable*)
- The Inspire11 conflict becomes unmanageable

**Keep going if:**
- You have 5+ clients who are happy
- You're growing, even slowly (1 new client/month average)
- You have an interesting pipeline (1-3 active prospects)
- The work is repetitive but not soul-crushing
- You can see a path to $10-15K MRR within 12 more months

## The Meta-Risk

The biggest risk isn't any of the above. It's **not starting at all** because the analysis is overwhelming.

If you read this whole kit and think "this is solid but I'll start next month" — that next month never comes.

The right move: skip the rest of the analysis. Set a Day 1 date (this Saturday). Do 5 walk-ins. See what happens. The market will tell you everything you need to know faster than any business plan.
